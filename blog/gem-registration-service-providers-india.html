GeM Registration for Service Providers: What Is Different from Product Sellers

Updated September 2026

Most content about GeM registration focuses on product sellers - manufacturers and traders who list physical goods on the portal. But a large portion of what the government buys through GeM is services - manpower supply, housekeeping, security, IT support, consultancy, transport, printing, and dozens of other categories. The step-by-step GeM registration process is the same starting point for service providers, but what comes after registration differs significantly from product sellers.

Does GeM Have Services?

Yes. GeM has a dedicated Services section that covers categories including:

  • Manpower services (skilled, semi-skilled, unskilled)
  • Housekeeping and facility management
  • Security services
  • IT services and software
  • Consultancy and advisory services
  • Transport and vehicle hiring
  • Printing and stationery services
  • Event management
  • Annual Maintenance Contracts (AMC)
  • Construction-related services (in some categories)

For a security agency that has a PSARA license, a transport operator, an IT services company, or a consultancy firm, GeM is a direct channel to government buyers who need exactly what these businesses provide.

How Service Provider Registration Differs from Product Registration

The basic registration steps - PAN, GST, Aadhaar, PFMS bank verification, and OLV - are the same for both product sellers and service providers. The complete document checklist for GeM registration covers what you need regardless of whether you are a product seller or service provider. The differences come in how you build your service catalog and how bids work.

Service Catalog Setup

Unlike a product listing where you enter physical specifications, a service catalog entry describes what you offer in terms of:

  • Service category and subcategory
  • Scope of service
  • Service delivery location (city, state, or pan-India)
  • Unit of measurement (per person per month, per vehicle per day, per hour, etc.)
  • Rate per unit

Getting the unit of measurement right matters for service listings the same way it matters for product bids - if a buyer's requirement is quoted in per-person-per-month and you list at a different unit, direct comparison breaks down and your listing may not appear in filtered searches.

Service Bids Are Almost Always Two-Packet

For most service categories, GeM uses the two-packet bid system - technical evaluation first, financial evaluation only after technical qualification. This means your technical bid document (describing your capability, experience, team, and compliance) must pass before your price is even looked at. The full breakdown of how to prepare a bid response that doesn't get disqualified is covered in the article on how to win orders on GeM.

Service bids commonly require:

  • Proof of business registration and relevant licenses (e.g., PSARA for security agencies)
  • Experience certificate or work orders from previous clients
  • List of qualified personnel
  • Financial documents showing turnover and net worth
  • GST returns for the past year or two

For new businesses or startups with DPIIT recognition, the prior experience and turnover requirements may be waived - the same exemptions described in the article on GeM advantages for MSME and startups apply to service bids as well.

Service Contracts and Order Management

Once you win a service order on GeM, the delivery mechanism is different from product dispatch. Instead of shipping goods, you submit service completion reports, attendance records, or work completion proofs depending on what the contract requires. The buyer confirms service delivery before payment is processed.

Some service contracts on GeM run for months or years - an Annual Maintenance Contract or a long-term manpower arrangement, for example. For these, your GeM profile and any linked license or certification must remain valid throughout the contract period.

Common Mistakes Specific to Service Providers

Not having the relevant licence listed on the profile. A security service listing without a PSARA license number, or an IT company without relevant empanelment details, fails technical evaluation even if everything else is in order.

Listing at a rate that doesn't reflect actual service costs. Service rates on GeM are visible to buyers and compared across sellers. Rates that are unrealistically low raise scrutiny during technical evaluation, while rates too high simply don't attract orders.

Ignoring bid alerts for service categories. Service bids are often time-sensitive and don't remain open long. A seller who checks GeM notifications once a week misses bids that close within 3 to 5 days of opening.

Not keeping GST returns current. Many service bids require submission of the latest GST returns as part of the financial qualification documents. An account with an ITR or GST return filing gap can be disqualified at the technical stage.

FAQs

Can a sole proprietor providing consultancy services register on GeM?

Yes. Sole proprietors providing eligible services can register on GeM as long as they have an active GST registration and meet the category-specific requirements.

Is there a minimum turnover required to register as a service provider on GeM?

GeM registration itself does not have a minimum turnover requirement. Individual service bids may have turnover thresholds in their technical criteria, though startups with DPIIT recognition are exempt from these.

Can the same GeM account be used for both products and services?

Yes. A single GeM seller account can have both product listings and service category listings. Many businesses sell both goods and services through the same account.

Do security agencies need to show their PSARA license on GeM?

Yes. When bidding for security service contracts on GeM, the PSARA license is typically a mandatory technical document in the bid response.
